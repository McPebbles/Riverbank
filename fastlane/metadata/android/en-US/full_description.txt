Riverbank is an independent, minimal client for the Amazon storefront, built for
GrapheneOS and hardened Android.

It does one thing: it renders the storefront's own mobile website inside a
locked-down WebView. On GrapheneOS that WebView is Vanadium, a Chromium build
with Google services, metrics and Safe Browsing reporting removed.

<b>What it does not have</b>

* No advertising ID, no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, no attribution SDK.
* No location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage or notification permission.
* No background services, no jobs, no wake locks, no widgets that phone home.
* No JavaScript bridge between the page and the app, so the site cannot reach
  Android APIs at all.
* No cloud backup: the manifest opts out of backup and device-to-device transfer.

The complete permission list is INTERNET and USE_BIOMETRIC. That is it.

<b>What it does have</b>

* A back arrow and the page title in the header — no address bar. Bottom
  navigation for Home, Cart, Orders, Lists and Account, a search icon that
  expands into a search field, and launcher shortcuts, so it behaves like an app
  rather than a bookmark.
* When a page is off the storefront, the header shows its host under the title,
  so a payment or sign-in redirect is never disguised as the store.
* The storefront's "get the app" banners and interstitials are filtered out of
  the page, and links that hand off to the native app are defused.
* A compiled-in tracker blocklist that drops known telemetry and ad endpoints,
  including the storefront's own beacons, with a counter you can inspect.
* A strict mode that refuses every subresource except the storefront and its
  image CDNs.
* Third-party cookies blocked by default; DNT and Sec-GPC sent on page loads.
* A reduced user agent, so the exact device model is not broadcast.
* Optional biometric or PIN lock, with the screen contents hidden from the
  recents view while the lock is on.
* Optional clearing of cookies and cache when the app closes.
* Invoice and document downloads through the system download manager, and photo
  uploads for reviews and returns through the system file picker, neither of
  which needs a storage permission.
* Payment and sign-in redirects stay inside the app, so 3-D Secure checkouts
  work; links that leave the storefront go to your browser.

<b>What it cannot do</b>

Riverbank is a website in a frame. It cannot deliver push notifications, scan
barcodes, use Alexa, stream Prime Video with DRM, or do anything else that
depends on the proprietary native app. If you need those, this is not for you.
